The passenger years

  1. Build a connection. Choose bus, rail or air, then two towns. Review the total and buy the route. You get infrastructure and one vehicle. Click towns on the map or use the dropdowns.
  2. Keep the seats full. Passengers accumulate at towns and board available services. Rivals draw from the same waiting pool. Longer trips earn higher fares; every vehicle has daily running costs.
  3. Grow sensibly. Services lets you add vehicles, change fares, pause a route or sell it. Higher fares reduce willingness to board. Airports and terminals are shared by your own routes in the same town.
  4. Mind the books. Company shows last month's profit, loans, objectives and standings. Borrow in £50,000 blocks up to £200,000; interest is charged monthly at 6% per year. No automatic bailout.
  5. Explore. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, click the minimap to travel. W A S D or arrow keys pan; Space pauses; 1/2/3 choose speed; Esc cancels map selection. On phones, use ☰ for management.
  6. Hear the network. Steam whistles and chuffs, bus engines and bells, and propeller drones are generated locally. Sounds favour nearby vehicles and stop while paused. Use ♫ to mute.
  7. Save your progress. Browser autosave runs every 30 seconds. Export a save from Company for a portable backup; browser storage can be unavailable in file previews.

PoC rules: fictional British geography and period-inspired vehicles, with illustrative game prices. Route construction is automatic, with simplified bridges, crossings and no signalling, collisions, cargo or passenger transfers. Vehicle technology stays in the 1950s. At the end of 1959 you receive a result. No external assets, accounts or downloads required.
