FIELD NOTES / HISTORICAL CONTEXT

A mountain. Many accounts.

This fly-through follows the Nepal / southeast-ridge route. The approach to the South Col happened over earlier days; the final summit push began around midnight on 9–10 May. This is an educational miniature, not a reconstruction of any one climber’s exact movements.

What is represented

Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse, the Khumbu Icefall, Western Cwm, Lhotse Face, Yellow Band, Geneva Spur, South Col, Balcony, South Summit and the pre-2015 Hillary Step. Summit elevation is shown as 8,848 m, the familiar figure used in 1996.

The terrain is procedurally modelled: distances and relief are compressed, camps are approximate, and tents and climbers are enlarged. The route is schematic, not survey data. Weather transitions and wind/radio sounds are illustrative, not recordings or measured conditions.

The three north-side deaths are acknowledged here; that separate route is not animated. Sherpa climbers and guides carried loads, fixed ropes and undertook rescue attempts at immense personal risk.

Sources and uncertainty

  • PBS NOVA · The Way to the Summit — route landmarks and terrain.
  • 1996 disaster timeline and its references — approximate sequence, checked against the disaster overview.
  • 1996 Mount Everest disaster — expeditions, rescue efforts and fatalities.
  • ITBP expedition — north-side context.

Into Thin Air (Jon Krakauer) and The Climb (Anatoli Boukreev and G. Weston DeWalt) offer differing eyewitness perspectives. Precise timings, decisions and responsibility remain disputed. This diorama avoids assigning a single cause or reconstructing uncertain deaths.
